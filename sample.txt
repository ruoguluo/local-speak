Local Speech, Read Aloud
========================

A note on what this file is for.

This sample exists so you can hear the pipeline without hunting for a document
of your own. It is plain text, so there is no layout to reconstruct, but it
still exercises the two things that break naive readers: hard line wrapping,
and the hy-
phenation that editors insert to justify a margin. The split word above should
come out as one word, while a compound like well-known should stay exactly as
it is, because its hyphen sits inside a line rather than at the end of one.

Every paragraph here is separated by a blank line, and the reader turns that
into a longer pause. If you listen closely you can hear the difference between
the end of a sentence and the end of a paragraph. That difference is most of
what makes a synthetic voice tolerable over twenty minutes instead of twenty
seconds.

Three things worth knowing before you press play:

- Phonon speaks English, French, German, Spanish, and Portuguese, and nothing else.
- The model is small enough to sit in memory beside everything else you run.
- Synthesis runs far faster than playback, so the audio is usually ready before you are.

One more paragraph, so the file ends on a complete thought rather than a list.
Reading long text out loud is an old idea that became unfashionable when
screens arrived. The interesting part is not that a machine can pronounce the
words. It is that the delay between wanting to hear something and hearing it
has collapsed to almost nothing, which changes when and where you are willing
to read at all.
